# Risk and Safety Margin

Part of the [game design](../game-design.md), whose Status says which of these rules are built. Balance figures here are examples; the game's values are in [public/data](../../public/data). Vocabulary follows [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## Risk and Safety Margin

- Each Mission is a sequence of Mission Events, and each Event has a failure probability.
- An Asset is delivered when its last Mission Event succeeds. When a Mission Event fails, Assets already delivered are safe and everything else is lost. A lander alone goes straight to entry/descent/landing, so carrying an orbiter as well is cheaper than two Missions but exposes the lander to orbit insertion.
- The game builds the list of Mission Events automatically from what the Mission carries. A lander or a rover adds entry/descent/landing, and Orbital refuelling adds a Rendezvous. The player then buys Safety Margin against that list.
- Safety Margin comes in three kinds:
  - Reliability (testing, redundancy) lowers an Event's failure probability.
  - Contingency (escape module, abort fuel) changes the outcome when an Event fails. Decision Points are often where a Contingency is used.
  - Protection (radiation shielding) slows the Health Crew lose to radiation, which builds up over the Mission's Duration and their time at Mars.
- A Contingency is a gamble, because a guaranteed rescue would always be the obvious answer. If its Mission Event fails, the player chooses at a Decision Point. Using it is a second roll, shown as a Risk Band: success saves every Asset not yet delivered with a shorter Service Life, and the Crew on board with less Health, and failure loses them and costs extra Public Support. Declining takes the ordinary loss. A Mission Event that fails with no Contingency bought raises no Decision Point. A rescued landing is a hard landing: it plays as a landing after the Decision Point, and earns first-landing Science and Milestones as any landing does. Launch has no Contingency yet.
- A Turn is worked out from the seed, the plan and the answers so far, and stops at the first unanswered Decision Point. Answering works it out again with that answer added, and doesn't re-roll anything else. Decision Points aren't saved, so a reload during one returns to the start of the Turn.
- All Safety Margin is worth having but expensive.
- Failure probabilities are shown to the player as rough bands (for example "~5%"), not exact figures.
- The random seed is fixed when the Program starts, so the same plan gives the same outcome after a reload. A changed plan can avoid a failure already seen, for example by buying more Reliability or by not launching. _Open: whether to keep this gap is to be settled by playtest feedback._
- Hazards are random environmental dangers, separate from planned Mission Events, that can raise Decision Points. Dust storms and solar particle events are the first two. A dust storm cutting solar power is planned.
- A dust storm can blow up over any Landing Site in a Turn, for a stretch of days. Whether it does, when and for how long come from the seed, the Turn and that site alone, so no plan or answer moves it. It raises a Decision Point only if an Asset or Crew are operating at that site when it starts: sheltering keeps the Assets and Crew there safe but earns no Ongoing Research for the storm's days, and carrying on keeps them earning with a chance of each Asset failing for good, and of each Crew member being hurt, which costs them Health. It costs no Public Support, an orbiter and Crew in Mars orbit aren't affected, and with nothing at stake it is only an entry in the events column.
- A solar particle event can hit a Mission on its way to Mars, so never one that has arrived, and never with no Mission in flight. Each day on the way carries the same chance, so a longer Trajectory is more exposed. They are meant to be rare and memorable rather than a routine interruption: about one Mission in twenty meets one, from about 4% on the 150-day Fast Trajectory to about 6% on the 210-day ones, which sets `chancePerDayInFlight` in [public/data/hazards.json](../../public/data/hazards.json). Safe mode costs each Asset on board some Service Life, and carrying on costs nothing unless they're hit, which shortens Service Life much further or loses the Mission and costs Public Support as a failed Mission Event does. Losing the Mission loses any Crew aboard, as losing Crew does, to the solar particle event.
- With Crew aboard, a solar particle event weighs their Health too. Safe mode costs each Crew member aboard a little Health, and carrying on costs them nothing unless the radiation hits, when each loses a large amount at once, the same roll that damages the Assets. Both amounts are in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json), and the Crew's Protection lessens both, as it does all radiation; the Decision Point says what each choice would cost them after it. The Health lost is a cause of its own in the Turn summary, and a Crew member it takes to zero dies on the way, as Health running out does.
- A landing due while a dust storm is over its Landing Site gets a go/no-go check, a Decision Point. Go lands now at a raised risk. Hold off lands on the day the storm has passed, at the usual risk, which moves the landing, the delivery, the research days and any Milestones with it, all within the Turn. Waiting costs each Asset being landed Service Life for each day held. Crew being landed use their Supplies for each day held, as they do every day from launch, so for them waiting costs the research those days would have earned, and Supplies if they were short: the check says so when the wait is longer than their Supplies last. There is no check for any other risk, such as landing at a site that hasn't been surveyed, because waiting does nothing for it. A Contingency for the landing still applies if it fails, whichever answer was given.
