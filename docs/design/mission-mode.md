# Mission Mode, saving, art and sound

Part of the [game design](../game-design.md), whose Status says which of these rules are built. Balance figures here are examples; the game's values are in [public/data](../../public/data). Vocabulary follows [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## Mission Mode

- A Turn takes about 20 minutes at 1x speed, with controls to speed it up to 5x, 10x or 20x, or to pause it. Pausing only affects how a Turn is shown and lasts only for that Turn, because choosing a speed is what ends it and the next Turn starts at the speed last chosen.
- The graphics are a mix of pre-made animations (such as an asteroid strike on a base) and displays (such as orbital tracking of a journey).
- Pre-made animations are scripted 3D sequences that run live in the game, not pre-rendered video, so they show the Assets the player actually bought at the Landing Site they actually chose.
- The first animation goal is one success and one failure animation per kind of Mission Event, and one per Hazard. More will follow. Each Mission Event, Milestone and Funding Threshold is added to an events column beside the view when it happens, with its date, title and description, newest at the top, and stays until the Turn ends. Nothing needs dismissing and time keeps running, so the player pauses to read. A failure stands out in the failure colour and plays its sound. A successful launch, Tanker launch or Mars ascent plays the launch sound, and a Rendezvous its own. Losing Crew, whatever the cause, plays a sound of its own, and so do Crew coming home safe.
- Play pauses at each Decision Point with no time limit. What raises one is under [Risk and Safety Margin](risk.md#risk-and-safety-margin).
- Mission Mode has four views, with smooth camera transitions between them:
  - Solar system: Earth, Mars and the Mission's journey along its Trajectory.
  - Mars orbit: orbiting Assets, arrival, aerocapture.
  - Surface: a Landing Site, with landings, rovers and bases.
  - Spacecraft: a close-up of the Mission in transit, used when something happens on the way: a solar particle event or a failed Mars transfer burn, and of the journey home's rendezvous in Mars orbit and Earth entry. A Mission with Orbital refuelling is shown in Earth orbit until it leaves for Mars, with its Tanker climbing from Earth, docking to refuel and pulling away, or colliding with it.
- The view switches automatically for Mission Events and Hazards, and the player can switch views at any time.
- A landing or crash plays for a few seconds of real time, the same at every speed, and pausing holds it. It only displays what has already been worked out. After a crash, the view goes back to whichever Landing Site still has something to show, or else to Mars orbit.
- A Crewed Mission has a ship of its own in every view: ringed in the solar system and in Mars orbit, a crew vehicle with a capsule in the spacecraft view, including in Earth orbit and through a solar particle event, and a lander of its own at a Landing Site. Its landing and crash are Crew landings and Crew crashes, which play as the others do. The Crew stay aboard until it touches down, then step out and work in front of it, moving about as their Activity has them do, or standing by it with nothing to do. They go indoors while they shelter from a dust storm, and each is gone from the day they're lost. Crew who land where Crew already are join them, coming down in a lander of their own beside the first, and each lander stands while any of the Crew it brought are there. Mission Mode moves to the surface for a Crew landing or crash, and to the spacecraft view for a solar particle event with Crew aboard, as it does without them.
- A Rendezvous, and the Tanker launch before it, play the same way. Once the Rendezvous has played, the view goes back to where it was before, or to the solar system if that was the spacecraft view and the Mission is lost.
- The journey home plays in the views in turn, each part for a few seconds of real time, the same at every speed, and pausing holds it. Each part waits for the one before it to finish playing, so at speed none is cut short. Mars ascent plays as a sequence of its own at the Landing Site it leaves: the Crew's cabin lifts off its descent stage, which stays standing, or the ascent vehicle's rocket off its platform, climbing out of the shot on its plume. A failed ascent cuts out, falls back and crashes, and the view stays on the wreck. Mission Mode then moves to the spacecraft view for the rendezvous in Mars orbit, where the cabin or the rocket's samples climb from Mars to dock with what waits to bring them home, the Earth return stage or the Earth return orbiter, or come in too fast, hit it and tumble away. Once it has played, the solar system view shows them heading back to Earth, from Mars on the day they leave orbit to Earth on the day of Earth entry, ringed if they are Crew. For Earth entry Mission Mode moves to the spacecraft view again: the capsule streaks down through the sky in the glow of entry, slows under its parachutes and splashes down in the ocean beside a recovery ship, or breaks up in the glow if its heat shield fails, and then the view goes back to the solar system. Crew who leave from Mars orbit fly no Mars ascent or rendezvous, so their journey starts in the solar system.
- The fuel plant, the habitat, the Mars lab and the ascent vehicle each stand on the surface as a model of their own, travelling in an aeroshell of their own colour, and the Earth return orbiter and a return vehicle, an Earth return stage, travel as models of their own. In Mars orbit, every Asset shows as a marker. Their Asset cards' pictures are drawn from these models.

## Saving

- The game saves automatically at the start of each Planning Mode.
- A few manual save slots are available, usable only in Planning Mode. Saving partway through Mission Mode isn't possible, so a reload always returns to the start of a Turn. What that can and can't undo is under [Risk and Safety Margin](risk.md#risk-and-safety-margin).

## Art, sound and onboarding

- Mars and the planets use NASA's public imagery and elevation data. Spacecraft use NASA's free 3D models where they fit, plus simple models made for the project. Plumes, dust and explosions are generated in code. Licensing rules are in [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-free-open-licensed-media-only.md).
- The first version has simple sound effects and no music.
- Every term has a tooltip, except in button labels.
- Tutorial mode is a guided tutorial the player can choose as a Program starts (see [Program, Turns and starting conditions](program.md#starting-conditions)). It leads them along one route, from a first orbiter through landing and Surveys, rovers and Research Grants, and developing the Meridian Colossus, to building a Base and landing Crew there. Coming home isn't part of it.

### Tutorial mode

- While Tutorial mode is on, each Planning Mode shows its advice at the top of the column beside the New Mission: what to plan this Turn, choice by choice, each with a sentence or two on why, so the player learns the reasoning rather than following orders. Terms in it have their tooltips. Above the suggestions it names the stage of the route the Program is at, with the whole route, so the player can see where they are heading.
- The advice is text only. No button fills in the plan, and the panel doesn't mark which suggestions the plan already matches: the player sets every control, and can ignore the advice.
- The advice is worked out afresh each Turn from the Program as it stands, by a tutorial strategy shaped like the balance runs' strategies (`src/tutorial`), so it recovers from failed Missions, lost Assets or Crew, Hazards and the player not following it. When it changes course, the panel says why before anything else, for example that the lander failed, so this Turn tries the same Landing Site again.
- At each Decision Point in Mission Mode, Tutorial mode suggests an answer with a short reason, beside the two choices, and outlines the choice it suggests. The player still answers it.
- The tutorial ends in one of two ways, shown once in a dialog as the Planning Mode where it happened starts. When Crew have landed at a Base, it says the goal is reached and sums up what the player learnt along the route. When the tutorial strategy finds that a Base can no longer be reached, by a rule the balance runs measure, it says what went wrong. Either way the Program carries on without advice: the advice panel and Decision Point suggestions stop, and stay stopped after saving and loading, because the Program records that the tutorial ended.
