# Crew

Part of the [game design](../game-design.md), whose Status says which of these rules are built. Balance figures here are examples; the game's values are in [public/data](../../public/data). Vocabulary follows [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## Crew

- Crew are named, with no individual traits apart from Health.
- The player chooses the Crew size for each Mission, trading more mass and Supplies against more research.
- Losing Crew causes a large drop in Public Support. The only Science cost is that the Ongoing Research they were doing stops.
- Crew don't have to come home. Staying at Mars harms them through radiation and other effects.
- Bringing Crew home safely earns a large amount of Public Support. Every safe return earns it, and the first return is also a Milestone worth more.
- A later Mission can fetch Crew already at Mars if it carries a return vehicle.
- Each Crew member has a single Health value. Radiation, time at Mars, Contingency rescues, solar particle events, carrying on through a dust storm and having no Supplies lower it, Protection and a habitat slow the decline, and at zero they die.
- Health is a whole number, from the most Crew launch with down to zero. How fast it falls, and what a rescue costs, are in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json). Radiation takes some for each day on the way to Mars, and on the way home, on a journey home or after a Free-return, so a Slow Trajectory costs more than a Fast one. At Mars, radiation and time at Mars each take some for every day there, carrying on from Turn to Turn, so Crew who reach Mars die there in the end. A Contingency that saves the Mission costs each Crew member on board a fixed amount at once, where it costs an Asset Service Life. Radiation is a cause of its own, so that Protection can slow just that.
- Each cause takes a whole point only once a whole point's worth has built up over the days, so the same days always cost the same Health. A Crew member dies on the first day their Health reaches zero, which counts as losing Crew, with its drop in Public Support, and stops their Ongoing Research from that day. That can happen on the way, if a long Trajectory and a rescue take them to zero: they don't arrive, or don't come home.
- Planning Mode shows each Crew member's Health, and the Health radiation on the way would leave a Crewed Mission's Crew as they arrive. The Turn summary says how much each Crew member lost and to what, whether they are still at Mars, died or came home.
- Crew need Supplies. When Supplies run out, Health falls quickly.
- Each Crewed Mission carries one of the Supplies on offer in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json), each with its mass and cost, which add to the Mission's. The least is what it carries unless the player chooses more. The data files also give how much each Crew member uses a day, so the same Supplies last a larger Crew fewer days, and Planning Mode shows each choice as the days it lasts the chosen Crew from launch. The plan names the Supplies by id, and the Simulation keeps what is left in kilograms.
- Supplies are used each day from launch: on the way to Mars, through any wait to land, on the way home, and at Mars, carrying on from Turn to Turn. Only Crew alive use them, so a Crew member who dies leaves more for the rest.
- The Crew in one place at Mars share one store of Supplies, as they share an Activity. Crew arriving where Crew already operate add the Supplies they have left to it, which can end a shortage there. A place whose Crew have all died or left takes its Supplies with it. Planning Mode shows the days the Supplies in each place will last the Crew there.
- The Supplies held in habitats at a Landing Site join the store of the Crew there for the Turn, from its start, or from the day a habitat lands. What is left at the end of the Turn fills the habitats there first, up to the Supplies they hold together, and the rest is the Crew's own. So the Crew draw on a habitat's Supplies once their own run out, and Crew landing there with Supplies of their own top the habitat up. The days Planning Mode shows for the Crew at a Landing Site count the Supplies in its habitats too.
- Supplies run out on the first day there isn't a whole day's worth left. From then until more arrive, each Crew member loses Health at the rate for having no Supplies, on top of everything else, as a cause of its own. The events column and the Turn summary say when Supplies ran out, on the way or in a place at Mars, and again on the first day of each Turn that Crew start with none.
- A Crew member who dies on a day their Supplies are out is lost to Supplies running out; one who dies while they still have Supplies is lost to their Health running out. Either way, losing them works as losing Crew does.
- Each Crewed Mission carries one of the levels of Protection on offer in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json), such as radiation shielding, each with its mass and cost, which add to the Mission's, and the share by which it slows radiation's harm. The first gives none and costs nothing, and is what it carries unless the player buys more. Each level slows radiation's harm by more than the one before, so more Protection is never worse. Planning Mode shows each level with that share, and the Health each would leave the Crew on arriving.
- Protection slows radiation in flight, on the way to Mars or home, the Health a solar particle event costs, and radiation at Mars, where it stays with the Crew from Turn to Turn. It slows nothing else: time at Mars, rescues, dust storms and having no Supplies cost as much with it as without. Each Crew member keeps the Protection their Mission carried, so Crew who join others at Mars each keep their own, and a Crew member whose level the data files no longer offer has none.
- The Turn summary says how much Health Protection saved each Crew member: how much more radiation and solar particle events would have taken over the same days with no Protection.
- A habitat ([Assets](assets.md)) operating at a Landing Site slows the Health the Crew there lose to time at Mars by its share, on each day it operates there, and slows nothing else: radiation, rescues, dust storms and having no Supplies cost as much beside it as without. Crew who land beside one, or beside whom one lands, are slowed from the day they are both there, and a habitat at another site does nothing for them. The Turn summary says how much Health it saved each Crew member, as for Protection: how much more time at Mars would have taken over the same days with no habitat.
- A Crewed Mission, with its Crew, Supplies and Protection, weighs more than the Meridian Heavy can carry, so it needs the Meridian Colossus, or a Launch Vehicle at least as large. Developing the Meridian Colossus is then the step towards Crew, rather than a bigger launcher with nothing to fill.
- A Crewed Mission is a Mission with Crew chosen for it, rather than an Asset: the plan names a Crew size, its Supplies and its Protection. The Crew sizes, Supplies and Protection on offer, what each costs and weighs, the ship that carries them, its Mission Events, the Crew's Health and Activities and their names are in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json). The Crew size's cost and mass add to the Mission's, which can carry Assets as well, within its Launch Vehicle's mass. Even the smallest Crew is too heavy for the Meridian Heavy.
- Crew aren't a technology, so they have no Technology Readiness, Service Life or Reliability in service: Health takes the place of Service Life. The Mission Events their ship brings, such as Mars orbit insertion, have Reliability and Contingency as any Mission Event does, and a Free-return is offered for that burn.
- Crew are named from the data files' list in order, and a name is never given twice in a Program, so a Crewed Mission needs enough names left for its Crew size.
- Crew arrive in Mars orbit, or land at their Landing Site, when the last of their ship's Mission Events succeeds, with the Health their flight left them, and stay there, operating on their own in later Turns. If a Mission Event fails first, they don't arrive.
- The Crew in one place share one Activity, kept from Turn to Turn until the player changes it, as an Asset's is. Crew arriving where Crew already operate take up their Activity, and otherwise the one the Crewed Mission chose. Each Crew member earns the Activity's Ongoing Research rate, so a larger Crew earns more, with its Public Support and any Research Grant, from the day they arrive. Benefits don't raise research in orbit.
- The first Crew to reach Mars earns a Milestone of its own. Even the smallest Crew earn more Public Support from Ongoing Research than any one Asset does.
- A Crewed Mission goes to Mars orbit, or its Crew land at a Landing Site chosen in Planning Mode, the Mission's one Landing Site, shared with any lander or rover it carries. Landing brings the Crew's ship through Mars orbit insertion and then entry/descent/landing, the Mission Events listed for a landing in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json), at the site's landing risk, with Reliability, Contingency, a Risk Band and the usual go/no-go check in a dust storm, as for any landing. Crew land at the site's own risk: they multiply it by nothing, so a landing with them is as risky as the riskiest Asset in it. A crash loses them, as losing Crew does, and a Contingency that saves the landing is a hard landing, which costs each of them Health as any rescue does.
- Crew at each Landing Site are a place of their own, apart from Crew in Mars orbit and at any other site, and share one Activity there, from the surface Activities in the data files. They earn its Ongoing Research for each Crew member, and a Benefit that raises research on the surface raises theirs too, as it does an Asset's there. Crew landing later at the same site take up the Activity of the Crew already there.
- The first Crew to land earn a Milestone of their own, on top of the one for the first Crew at Mars, which landing Crew earn too. Crew landing at a site nothing has landed at earn its first-landing Science, as any landing does. First landing on Mars stays the Milestone for the first Asset to land.
- A dust storm over Crew at their Landing Site raises the same Decision Point as for Assets there. Sheltering earns them nothing for the storm's days. Carrying on keeps them earning, and each Crew member has the chance in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json) of being hurt on a day during the storm, which costs the Health it gives there, as a cause of its own. Health running out that way loses them as Health running out does.
- A Mission Event that fails for good with Crew still aboard loses them, as it loses the Assets not yet delivered. Launch has no Contingency yet, so a failed launch kills the Crew. A solar particle event that loses the Mission loses its Crew too.
- Losing Crew costs Public Support for each Crew member lost, set in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json), on the day they're lost, on top of what the failure itself costs. It never takes Public Support below zero, so it can take all that's left. A Decision Point that could lose Crew says what losing them would cost. The events column and the Turn summary name each Crew member lost, as failures.
- Lost Crew earn no Ongoing Research from the day they're lost, and Crew already at Mars carry on. Their names are never given again. Whatever loses Crew, it works the same way, and the Program records why: a failed Mission Event, a solar particle event, Health running out, or Supplies running out.
- If the arrival burn fails on a Free-return, the Crew come home alive with the Mission, with the Health they have left, and earn what any Crew home safe earn (below). The events column and the Turn summary name them. Crew who are home aren't flown again, and their names aren't given again.
- The Program saves every Crew member who has flown, with their name, Health and where they are: at Mars, in Mars orbit or at a Landing Site, with their arrival date, lost, with the day and why, or home, with the day they got back. It also saves the Protection each one's Mission carried, the Activity of the Crew in each place, and the Supplies left there. A save from before Crew upgrades with none.

## Coming Home

- A Crewed Mission to Mars orbit can make a round trip: it carries an Earth return stage, whose cost and mass, the same for any Crew size, are in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json) and add to the Mission's. Planning Mode offers it as a choice on the Crewed Mission, saying what it adds. Crew who land can't make a round trip until they can leave the surface, in a later ticket, so the choice goes with landing.
- The journey home has Mission Events of its own, listed for the round trip in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json), such as trans-Earth injection as the Crew leave Mars orbit and Earth entry at the end. Each is timed from the day the journey leaves Mars, which the data files also give, and the last of them ends before the Turn does. Each has Reliability and a Contingency, as any Mission Event does, and both are bought with the round trip, at launch, in the Mission Events step, where the journey home's Mission Events follow those of the way there. A Free-return isn't offered for them.
- The Earth return stage arrives in Mars orbit with its Crew, and waits there with the Reliability and Contingencies bought for their journey. The Program keeps it until it has brought them home or none of them are left at Mars. Crew who went one way have none.
- On any later Turn, Planning Mode lists a choice to bring round-trip Crew in Mars orbit home with their Crew at Mars, saying when they would leave and get home, as an Asset's mission can be ended. At most one journey home flies each Turn, with or without a new Mission, and choosing it costs nothing more that Turn.
- The round-trip Crew stay at Mars, working and losing Health as Crew there do, until the day the journey leaves, and earn nothing from then on. Those still alive then leave together, with their share of the Supplies left in Mars orbit: as much as each Crew member who stays keeps, so Crew who all leave take everything. Crew who die before the day they would leave are lost at Mars, as Crew there are, and don't go.
- On the way home, radiation and having no Supplies lower Health as on the way to Mars, with each Crew member's own Protection slowing the radiation, and a Contingency rescue costs Health as any does. Each Mission Event is rolled as any is, and one that fails for good, after any Contingency, loses the Crew aboard, costing its own Public Support and that for losing Crew. Crew whose Health runs out on the way are lost then, to their Health, or to their Supplies if those had run out. Losing them works as losing Crew does.
- Crew who come home safe, from a journey home or a Free-return, earn the Public Support set for each Crew member in [public/data/crew.json](../../public/data/crew.json) on the day they get home, and the first Crew home earn the First Crew home Milestone, which is worth more. The events column says who came home and what it earned, and the Turn summary names them, with the Public Support, and the Health each lost on the way after what they lost at Mars. A journey home that fails is a section of the Turn summary of its own.
- Mission Mode shows the journey home in its existing views: the Crew's ship leaves Mars orbit on the day they go, and the events column lists the journey's Mission Events, any Decision Point and their homecoming. Its own visuals are a separate ticket.
- The Program saves each Earth return stage at Mars, with the day it arrived, the Crew it is for and what was bought for their journey. A save from before round trips upgrades with none, so every Crew member at Mars in it went one way.
